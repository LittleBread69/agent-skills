# Deep Research

Step 1. Restate the question in one sentence and split it into 3 to 6 sub-questions. If the request is ambiguous and a wrong guess would waste a lot of time, ask one short clarifying question first.

Step 2. Search each sub-question separately with short queries (2-6 words). Do not combine several topics in one query. Do at least one search per sub-question, and more when results are thin or conflicting.

Step 3. Open the most promising pages with the fetch/read-URL tool instead of relying on snippets. Prefer original sources (official sites, documentation, papers, government pages) over blogs and aggregators.

Step 4. Take notes as you go: claim, source URL, date. Keep numbers, names and dates exactly as the source gives them.

Step 5. Before writing, check what is still missing or contradictory. Run extra searches to settle conflicts, and rule out alternative answers instead of only collecting support for the first one you found.

Step 6. Write the report in your own words with a short summary at the top, then one section per sub-question, then open questions or uncertainties. Use a table only when comparing items on the same criteria.

Step 7. End with a "Sources:" list of URLs. Mark anything you could not verify as unverified. Never fill a gap from memory without saying so.

If sub-agents are available, you may hand independent sub-questions to them, but do the final writing yourself.
