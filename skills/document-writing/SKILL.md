# Document Writing

Step 1. Before writing, fix four things: who will read it, what they should know or do afterwards, the format (email, report, README, essay), and the length. If one of these is unknown and matters, ask one short question; otherwise pick a sensible default and say which.

Step 2. Lead with the main point or the decision needed. Put background after it, not before.

Step 3. Structure: a short title, a one or two sentence summary, then sections with plain headings. Use bullet points only for lists of parallel items; write reasoning as normal paragraphs.

Step 4. Use plain, concrete language. Prefer short sentences, active voice and exact numbers and names. Cut filler such as "it is important to note that" and repeated points.

Step 5. Never invent facts, quotes, figures or sources to make the text sound complete. If a detail is missing, leave a clearly marked placeholder like [date] or ask for it.

Step 6. Match the requested tone: formal for official letters, direct and friendly for chat or team messages. For emails include a subject line and a clear closing request.

Step 7. Write in the language the user wrote in, unless they ask for another one.

Step 8. Finish by checking the draft against the Step 1 list: right reader, main point first, right length. Then deliver the text only, with at most one line of comment afterwards.
